Background
Why Are Newborns Vitamin K Deficient?

Poor Placental Transfer

Vitamin K does not cross the placenta efficiently. Cord blood levels are barely detectable regardless of maternal intake.

Low Breast Milk Content

Breast milk: 1–4 µg/L. Formula: 50–60 µg/L (15–60× more).

Sterile Gut

Newborns lack intestinal bacteria that synthesise vitamin K₂. Colonisation takes weeks to months.

Limited Stores

Neonatal liver vitamin K stores are extremely low. Half-life in neonatal plasma ≈ 70 hours.

Shearer MJ. Blood Rev. 2009;23(2):49-59 · Lippi G & Franchini M. Blood Transfus. 2011;9(1):4-9
UK Formulation
Konakion MM Paediatric
Phytomenadione 2 mg/0.2 mL · IM: 1 mg at birth (single dose) · Oral: 2 mg at birth, 4–7 days, 1 month (breastfed)
"MM" = Mixed Micelles. Developed by Roche in 1995 to replace the Cremophor EL-based formulation. Uses natural bile salt + lecithin to dissolve fat-soluble vitamin K in water, specifically designed to reduce anaphylaxis risk.
Postmarketing surveillance (Pereira 1998): 85 anaphylactoid reactions (6 fatal) with old formulation vs 1 non-fatal with Konakion MM, across ~1.3 billion treated.
Pereira SP et al. Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf. 1998;7(3):173-82 · Tripp JH & McNinch AW. Arch Dis Child. 2007;92:F759-66
UK Formulation
Ingredients Explained

Phytomenadione (Vitamin K₁)

The active ingredient. Identical to naturally occurring vitamin K₁ found in green leafy vegetables.

Glycocholic acid

A naturally occurring bile salt. Your baby's body produces this. Used as emulsifier. Produced synthetically.

Lecithin

A phospholipid found in egg yolks and soya. The other half of the mixed micelle system. Present in everyday foods.

Sodium hydroxide / Hydrochloric acid

pH adjustment only. Present in trace amounts. Your stomach produces HCl naturally.

Water

For injection.

Debunking
"Vitamin K Causes Leukaemia" — The Original Study
Golding et al. 1992 (BMJ): Case-control study, 2 Bristol hospitals. 195 cases vs 558 controls. Reported ~2× leukaemia risk with IM vitamin K (OR 1.97, 95% CI 1.3–3.0). Caused widespread alarm and policy changes across the UK.
Methodological problems identified:
1. Small sample from only 2 hospitals, limited generalisability
2. Vitamin K exposure often assumed, not documented — data clerks used the nearest baby's record or asked staff to recall policy from years earlier
3. Data collectors not blinded to case/control status
4. Only ~50% of eligible cases included
5. Pooled analysis OR dropped to 1.06 when Bristol data excluded
Golding J et al. BMJ. 1992;305:341-6 · Roman E et al. Br J Cancer. 2002;86:63-9
Debunking
The Cancer Link: Subsequent Evidence
Every major follow-up study failed to confirm Golding's findings.
1992 — Golding et al. (BMJ) · 195 cases
The original study. Methodological problems identified.
1993 — Sweden (Ekelund) · 1,384,424 infants
OR 1.01 all cancers, 0.90 leukaemia. No association.
1993 — USA (Klebanoff, NEJM) · 54,795
No increased risk. US incidence unchanged 1948–1980 despite near-universal adoption.
1996 — Germany (von Kries) · 272 cases
Population-based case-control in Lower Saxony. No association.
2002 — Pooled analysis (Roman) · 2,431 cases, 6,338 controls
OR 1.06 when Bristol excluded. No convincing evidence.
2003 — UK Childhood Cancer Study · 2,530 cases, 4,487 controls
National study. No association between IM vitamin K and leukaemia or any cancer.
A concerning signal was investigated thoroughly and not confirmed.
Debunking
"Breast Milk / Cord Clamping / Diet Can Replace It"
"Breast milk is enough" — Breast milk: 1–4 µg/L. Formula: 50–60 µg/L. 87.7% of VKDB cases in Japanese surveillance were exclusively breastfed. Colostrum is not rich in vitamin K.
"Delayed cord clamping" — Beneficial for iron and haemoglobin. Not beneficial for vitamin K. Vitamin K is barely detectable in cord blood. DCC gives more blood, but it's still vitamin K-deficient.
"Maternal diet" — Systematic review (6 RCTs, >21,000 women): no difference in newborn bleeding. Maternal 5 mg/day (800% RDA) raises infant levels only to near formula-fed. Vitamin K doesn't cross the placenta efficiently.
All three are good practices. None replaces vitamin K prophylaxis. They address different problems.

Debunking
"The Dose is 20,000 Times What an Adult Needs"
The claim: Parents compare the 1 mg neonatal dose to the adult Adequate Intake (~90 µg/day) on a per-kilogram basis, arriving at dramatic multiples. This conflates dietary reference intake with a therapeutic dose.
The evidence:
• No established Tolerable Upper Intake Level for vitamin K₁ (IOM: insufficient evidence of toxicity at any studied dose)
• Hyperbilirubinaemia observed only at doses of 25–30 mg (25–30× the prophylactic dose)
• The dose is high relative to dietary intake, but the therapeutic window is wide and safety data spans 60+ years.
The Communication Problem
Why Facts Alone Don't Work
Correcting misinformation with facts can paradoxically increase resistance. Nyhan et al. (2014): debunking the autism–vaccine link may actually decrease intent to vaccinate.
The JITSUVAX framework identifies 11 attitude roots from 152 studies and 2,414 anti-vaccination arguments. Surface claims are symptoms — address the root, not just the symptom.

Fear & Phobias

Toxins, heavy metals, DNA alteration, immune overload

Distorted Risk

Vaccination riskier than disease, unnecessary

Religious Concerns

Prohibited materials, against natural/divine order

Worldview

Naturalistic thinking, civil liberties

Epistemic Relativism

"Mother knows best," alternative health

Distrust

Experimental, biased data, authorities overreacting

Unwarranted Beliefs

Implausible side effects, natural remedies better

Reactance

Refusal as empowered choice, anti-paternalism

Adapted from JITSUVAX (Lewandowsky et al.) · Nyhan et al. Pediatrics. 2014;133(4)
Communication Framework
Empathetic Refutational Interview (ERI)
Outperformed motivational interviewing for shifting vaccine attitudes (Holford et al. 2025, npj Vaccines).
1
Elicit
Identify the attitude root. Open-ended questions + active listening.
"Do you mind sharing your thoughts on vitamin K?"
2
Affirm
Build trust through empathy. Acknowledge the legitimacy of the underlying motivation. Skip this and the rest fails.
"That makes complete sense — that's good parenting."
3
Correct
Replace the misconception. Tailored refutation that respects the attitude root.
"Vitamin K isn't a chemical — it's a natural vitamin your baby needs but can't yet make enough of."
4
Inform
Facts at the right moment. Only after trust + correction. Delivered too early, facts bounce off.
The incidence data, the risk of ICH, the 81× relative risk.

Practical Guidance
When the Conversation Doesn't Change Their Mind
Not every conversation will result in acceptance. That is not a failure. Research shows that having the conversation does not entrench refusal — you haven't made things worse.
Offer oral alternative
Oral Konakion MM keeps the baby partially protected. Explain limitations honestly — it's far better than nothing.
Document thoroughly
Record what was discussed, offered (IM and oral), declined, and that risks including ICH and death were explained.
Leave the door open
"I respect your decision. The offer is always open. If you change your mind, just let us know."
Look after yourself
These conversations are emotionally taxing. Debrief with colleagues.
Summary
Key Takeaways
  • 83% encounter vitamin K refusal at least monthly; 50% have felt unable to adequately respond
  • The UK uses Konakion MM — no benzyl alcohol, no polysorbate 80, no black box warning. Most online concerns are about US products
  • The cancer link (Golding 1992) has been thoroughly refuted by every major follow-up study
  • IM is gold standard. Oral is a reasonable alternative if IM is declined
  • Facts alone don't work — identify the attitude root and use ERI
  • Parents have the right to refuse. Your job: inform clearly, offer alternatives, document, leave the door open
